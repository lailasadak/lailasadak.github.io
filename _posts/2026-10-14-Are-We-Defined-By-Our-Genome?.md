We cannot say that we are entirely defined by our genome but we can say that it is a factor along with epigenetics. We inherit genes but those genes are able to turn on or off given the environment we interact with. Environmental factors such as diet, stress, and lifestyle all have an effect on how our body processes the information it is receiving. A great example is identical twins. Although they have the exact same genetic makeup, but their genes turn on and off differently because they are still two different humans. Their DNA sequence is not being manipulated in anyway; the way each of them experience and explore life is what differentiates them. Another reason we are not completely defined by our genome is because we actually share about 99.9% of the same genetic code. The small .1% is actually the variation that humans have amongst one another from things such as culture and tradition. These ultimately affect our epigenetics because our daily life brings out certain genes we are predisposed to. If we are only different by a fraction, how can we only be defined by our genome. This is just touching the surface but when we dig deeper, we will find that a lot more is involved. An example is the human microbiome. The microbiome is the collection of all microbes and their genetic material that naturally live in and on our bodies. It is also known as our second genome. Our microbiome has to change quickly and constantly based on multiple factors like diet and environment. These can influence your physical, emotional, social health easily which in turn effects your epigenetics. There has also been the rise of comparative genomic projects that indicate that humans are defined just as much by the DNA we have lost as the DNA we have. Studies have shown that humans are lacking at least 10,000 bits of DNA in comparison to their other mammal counterparts parts. These deletions have removed genetic off switches which has allowed our brains to grow and develop more cognitive. These structures are uniquely to us and shows that our genome isn’t as strict as thought. The idea that our genome defines us is understandable in theory because some look to purely science for a explanation to things but while that is is true, the fact that our environment plays a big role is undeniable. The way we interact with others and the way they interact with you go hand in hand in this question. We also need to realize that just because we inherit genes or you have a family history of a certain disease doesn’t mean that you are 100% going to get it. Yes, you might have a higher chance but those genes might not turn on depending on what you do or not do. That individuality is why we are not defined by our genome alone. Rather than saying that we are only defined by our genome, we can recognize that both our genome and epigenetics work together to make us who we are.
